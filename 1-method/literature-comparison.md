# Colometric Methodology: Literature Comparison

## Our Four-Criteria Hierarchy

1. **Syntax (floor)** — never violate
2. **Atomic thought (primary)** — governs when criteria conflict
3. **New mental image (strong signal)** — operates within syntactic permission
4. **Natural breath pause (tiebreaker)** — secondary, decides ambiguous cases

## Comparison with Published Scholarship

### Priscille Marschall (2023) — "Refining the Criteria for Delineating Cola and Periods"
- **Source:** Graeco-Roman rhetorical treatises (Aristotle, Pseudo-Demetrius, Cicero, Quintilian)
- **Key concept:** "semantico-syntactic completeness" — the colon must convey a "complete thought"
- **This IS our atomic thought test**, arrived at from a different tradition
- **Novel contribution:** phrases without verbs CAN be cola if they carry enough semantic weight
- **Validates:** our short emphatic lines ("every man alone," "but with real intent") — these are classical *commata*, legitimate short cola that create "stabs" of emphasis
- **Challenges:** our occasional requirement that a line contain a verb
- **Gap:** no hierarchy among criteria; no image test; no BOM application
- **PDF saved at:** `research/Marschall-2023.pdf`

### Royal Skousen — "The Earliest Text" (Yale, 2009/2022)
- **Criteria:** Phrases/clauses (syntax) + "reasonable pause points" (breath)
- **No formal hierarchy** — relies on editorial judgment
- **Closest to:** our syntax (floor) + breath (tiebreaker), but without atomic thought or image
- **Weakness:** non-replicable; criteria unstated; no validation methodology
- **Strength:** deep linguistic expertise; access to original manuscript evidence

### Donald Parry — "Poetic Parallelisms" (FARMS, 1992/2007)
- **Single criterion:** parallelism detection (14+ categories)
- **Fundamentally different:** identifies macro-level literary structures, not line-by-line colometry
- **Our overlap:** Rule 13 (parallel structures stack) and Rule 20 (polysyndetic stacking)
- **Our advantage:** finer granularity; we operate within and between parallel structures

### M.P. O'Connor — "Hebrew Verse Structure" (1980)
- **Strongest analog** to our syntax-first principle
- **Six syntactic constraints** define the Hebrew poetic line
- **Rejects meter/rhythm** as criteria — syntax is definitional
- **Gap:** no cognitive/perceptual criteria (no image test, no breath test)
- **Key validation:** his approach supports our syntax-as-floor principle

### Thomas Renz — "Colometry and Accentuation" (2003)
- **Empirical finding:** Masoretic accent divisions align with syntactic boundaries in **92% of cases**
- **Supports:** our placement of syntax as the floor — it IS the dominant criterion even in Hebrew poetry
- **Inapplicable directly** (no accent tradition for BOM), but methodologically validating

### Muilenburg, Freedman, Berlin
- Operate at higher levels of abstraction (literary-unit delimitation, syllable statistics, multi-level parallelism)
- Not directly comparable to our line-by-line colometric method

## Key Findings

1. **No published scholar has a formal, ranked hierarchy** for BOM colometric criteria. Our four-criteria hierarchy is novel.
2. **Syntax-first is validated** across traditions (O'Connor, Renz, Marschall all converge on syntax as foundational).
3. **"Atomic thought" = "semantico-syntactic completeness"** (Marschall's term for the same concept).
4. **The image test has no direct parallel** in published colometric scholarship — this is our original contribution.
5. **Short emphatic lines (commata)** are validated by classical rhetorical theory (Marschall, citing Cicero, Pseudo-Demetrius).
6. **Our method is more operationally precise** than any published alternative — four ranked criteria with specific rules, mechanical validation (0.25% error rate), and empirical voice-differentiation confirmation.

## Strengths of Our Method
- Explicit hierarchy resolves criterion conflicts
- Mechanical rules (syntactic bonds) are falsifiable
- Empirically validated (voice differentiation, error-rate testing)
- Combines ancient insight (syntax, breath) with modern cognitive science (image, atomic thought)
- Applied at scale (6,603 verses, 15 books)

## Weaknesses / Areas to Strengthen
- Less historically grounded than Marschall's Graeco-Roman analysis
- No formal syllable-count constraints (we use "breath" informally)
- The image test needs theoretical grounding — could cite Chafe (1994) on intonation units as "idea units"
- We haven't formally distinguished "autonomous" vs. "periodic" cola (Marschall's distinction)
