## Preliminary: Key Concepts

### Is "discourse formula" a recognized linguistic category?

**PARTIALLY.** The exact term is not standard, but maps onto two established categories:

1. **Formulaic sequences** (Wray 2002): sequences "stored and retrieved whole from memory at the time of use, rather than being subject to generation or analysis by the language grammar." Speech attribution formulas, editorial narrator formulas, and sermonic address patterns are textbook examples.

2. **Discourse markers** (Schiffrin 1987; Fraser 1999): expressions that signal relationships between discourse units. "And it came to pass," "And now," "Wherefore," "For behold" qualify.

**Recommendation:** Use "formulaic sequence" (Wray 2002) for the stored-and-retrieved-whole property, and "discourse marker" (Schiffrin 1987) for the discourse-structuring function.

### Does the public/private verb distinction (Quirk et al.) map onto break patterns?

**No — and our own data confirms this.** Quirk et al. (1985, §16.31–16.35) classify verbs taking that-clause complements into PUBLIC (say, declare, announce), PRIVATE (think, believe, know), SUASIVE (recommend, suggest), and EMOTIVE (regret, marvel). Our March 29 audit documented that a verb-type classification failed empirically: the pattern is governed by breath-unit length and formulaic status, not verb semantics. This is an honest finding worth citing as evidence of empirical grounding.

### Does prosodic constituency support the "atomic breath unit" concept?

**CONFIRMED.** The prosodic hierarchy (Nespor & Vogel 1986; Selkirk 1984, 2011) defines the **intonational phrase** (IP) as a prosodic constituent bounded by potential pause sites, roughly corresponding to a clause or major phrase. Chafe (1994) defines "intonation units" as the basic units of spoken language production, each containing one new idea. Our "atomic breath unit" corresponds to the intonational phrase / intonation unit.

### Does information structure support break patterns?

**CONFIRMED.** Halliday's (1967, 1994) Theme/Rheme distinction maps onto our breaks:
- **FEFs as marked Themes**: scene-setting material that must complete before the Rheme (main predication) begins
- **Fronted adverbials**: textbook marked Theme; breaking after respects Theme/Rheme boundary
- **Cataphoric vs. anaphoric "that" clauses**: maps onto Given/New information structure (Chafe 1994)

## Rule-by-Rule Audit

### Rule 1: Wayyehi Rule — **CONFIRMED**
"And it came to pass that" stays on one line.
- **Principle:** Formulaic sequence integrity; discourse marker unity
- **Citations:** Wray 2002 (formulaic sequences); Joüon & Muraoka 2006 §166 (wayyehi construction); Waltke & O'Connor 1990 §33.2.1–33.2.3; Andrason 2016 (wayyehi+T+main-verb schema)

### Rule 2: "Expedient That" — **CONFIRMED**
"It is expedient that" is a fixed idiom — don't break at "that."
- **Principle:** Extraposed subject with adjectival complement
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 11, 16 (extraposition); Quirk et al. 1985 §16.72–16.73 (anticipatory "it")

### Rule 3: Rhetorical Address Formulas — **CONFIRMED**
"And now, O king," stays as one unit.
- **Principle:** Vocative as syntactically independent clause-peripheral element
- **Citations:** Hill 2014 (VocP); Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 10 (supplements); Quirk et al. 1985 §10.52–10.53

### Rule 4: Circumstantial Clause Pairing — **CONFIRMED**
Paired phrases describing the same condition stay together.
- **Principle:** Co-predicating circumstantial adjuncts within single experiential process
- **Citations:** Halliday 1994 (circumstantial adjuncts); Selkirk 2011 (single intonational phrase)

### Rule 5: Equivalence Restatement — **CONFIRMED**
When "or" introduces a restatement, keep it with the restated material.
- **Principle:** Appositive coordination; reformulation marker
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 15 ("appositive or"); Quirk et al. 1985 §13.103; Blakemore 1993 (reformulation)

### Rule 6: Line Length as Signal, Not Rule — **CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Prosodic variability serves discourse function
- **Citations:** Chafe 1994 (intonation unit length varies by discourse type); Muilenburg 1969 (rhetorical structure, not line length, determines boundaries); O'Connor 1980 (syntactic constraints over syllable counts)

### Rule 7a: Causal Clauses Break — **CONFIRMED**
"Because" introduces a new line.
- **Principle:** Clause boundary at causal subordination; adverbial clause is syntactically optional (adjunct, not complement)
- **Citations:** Quirk et al. 1985 §15.45; Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 11; Selkirk 2011

### Rule 7b: Purpose Clauses Break — **CONFIRMED**
"That they might / that ye may" introduces a new line.
- **Principle:** Adverbial clause of purpose; syntactic adjunct introducing new proposition
- **Citations:** Quirk et al. 1985 §15.48; Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 11

### Rule 8: Framing Devices Attach — **CONFIRMED**
"For behold," "Wherefore," "And now" attach to what they introduce.
- **Principle:** Discourse marker scope; textual Theme requires Rheme
- **Citations:** Schiffrin 1987 (discourse markers); Halliday 1994 (textual Theme); Fraser 1999

### Rule 9: No Dangling Conjunctions — **CONFIRMED**
Never end a line on "and," "or," "but," "nor."
- **Principle:** Conjunction syntactically dependent on right conjunct
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 15; Selkirk 2011

### Rule 10: Verb-Object Bond — **CONFIRMED**
Never split verb from direct object on short phrases.
- **Principle:** Head-complement bond within VP
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 4; Quirk et al. 1985 §2.13; Nespor & Vogel 1986
- **Note:** Long objects with internal structure can legitimately begin new intonational phrase

### Rule 11: No Dangling Articles — **CONFIRMED**
Never end a line on an article.
- **Principle:** Determiner-noun constituency (DP)
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 5; Abney 1987

### Rule 12: Auxiliary-Verb Integrity — **CONFIRMED**
Never split auxiliary from main verb.
- **Principle:** Verbal group integrity
- **Citations:** Halliday 1994 (verbal group); Quirk et al. 1985 §3.21; Nespor & Vogel 1986

### Rule 13: Parallel Structures Stack — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
Mirror parallel elements across lines.
- **Principle:** Iconic representation of paradigmatic equivalence
- **Citations:** Kugel 1981 (A, and what's more, B); Lowth 1753; O'Connor 1980; Aristotle *Rhetoric* III.9 (isocolon)
- **Caveat:** Overgenerates without register/image qualifier. The compound list break signals refinement (elided auxiliary, possessive restart, demonstrative, relative clause) is necessary.

### Rule 14: Escalatory vs. Restrictive Qualifying Phrases — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Given/New distinction; supplementary vs. integrated modification
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 12 (integrated/supplementary); Lambrecht 1994 (focus structure); Prince 1981 (Given/New)
- **Caveat:** "Escalatory" is not standard terminology. Better: "supplementary appositive with New information content" vs. "integrated modifier with Given/identifying function."

### Rule 15: Vocative Indivisibility — **CONFIRMED**
"O Lord God" stays whole.
- **Principle:** VocP has internal syntactic structure that must complete
- **Citations:** Hill 2014

### Rule 16: Dangling "That" — **CONFIRMED**
Break BEFORE "that" so it leads the next line.
- **Principle:** Complementizer belongs to the clause it introduces (head of CP)
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002 (binder); Selkirk 2011 (complementizers procliticize to following IP)

### Rule 17: "Caused That" Integrity — **CONFIRMED**
"Caused" requires its complement clause.
- **Principle:** Obligatory clausal complement of causative verb
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 14; Quirk et al. 1985 §16.38–16.39

### Rule 18: Fixed Idiom Integrity — **CONFIRMED**
"Put to death," "from time to time," "prevailed upon" are indivisible.
- **Principle:** Phraseological fixedness
- **Citations:** Wray 2002; Cowie 1998 (*Phraseology*, Oxford UP)

### Rule 19: Cataphoric "That" Breaks; Anaphoric Merges — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Given/New information structure
- **Citations:** Halliday & Hasan 1976 (*Cohesion in English*); Prince 1981; Chafe 1994
- **Caveat:** Our use of "cataphoric" stretches slightly beyond standard referential meaning. More precisely: that-clauses with New information content break; those with Given information content merge.

### Rule 20: Compound List Break Signals — **CONFIRMED** (Mar 29 formulation)
Breaks within compound lists require grammatical signals: elided auxiliary, possessive restart, demonstrative, relative clause.
- **Principle:** Structural ellipsis and constituency
- **Citations:** Aristotle *Rhetoric* III.12 (polysyndeton/asyndeton); Fahnestock 1999 (*Rhetorical Figures in Science*, Oxford UP)
- **Note:** Earlier register-based formulation would rate PARTIALLY CONFIRMED; the grammatical-signal formulation is falsifiable and mechanically testable.

### Rule 21: "According to" — Manner vs. Source/Authority — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Adjunct type distinction
- **Citations:** Quirk et al. 1985 §8.78–8.86; Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 8
- **Caveat:** Line between manner and source has inherent fuzziness.

### Rule 22: Divine Title Appositives — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Focus-bearing supplementary appositive (breaks) vs. Given-information identifying appositive (merges)
- **Citations:** Lambrecht 1994; Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 5

### Rule 23: Narrowing Vocatives — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Vocative scope narrowing / specificational focus
- **Citations:** Hill 2014 (vocative syntax); Lambrecht 1994 (topic narrowing)
- **Note:** The unidirectional pattern (15 instances, all broad→narrow, never reversed) is an **original empirical finding** that contributes to scholarship.

### Rule 24: Definitional "Which Is" — Equation vs. Description — **CONFIRMED**
- **Principle:** Specificational vs. attributive predication
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 12; Higgins 1979 (*The Pseudocleft Construction in English*)

### Rule 25: "And Thus We See" — **CONFIRMED but OVERRIDDEN**
- **Principle:** Metatextual discourse marker / narratorial intrusion
- **Citations:** Hyland 2005 (*Metadiscourse*); Genette 1980 (*Narrative Discourse*); Schiffrin 1987
- **Status as of 2026-03-29:** Linguistically valid as a discourse-marker break, but OVERRIDDEN by the foundational test. "We see" without its complement is an incomplete thought. When discourse-marker status and syntactic completeness conflict, syntactic completeness governs unless the prosodic break is doing clear, significant work. The "and thus we see" break is a small prosodic pause that doesn't change meaning or reveal hidden structure. **Rule 25 eliminated; these constructions now governed by the foundational test.**

### Rule 26: Adjective + "That" Complement — **CONFIRMED**
"Possible that," "expedient that," "desirous that" stay together.
- **Principle:** Obligatory clausal complement of predicate adjective
- **Citations:** Huddleston & Pullum 2002, Ch. 14; Noonan 2007 (complement-taking predicates)

## Summary

**20 CONFIRMED, 7 PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, 0 UNCONFIRMED, 0 PROBLEMATIC.**

The PARTIALLY CONFIRMED rules are all cases where the linguistic principle is real but our formulation uses non-standard terminology or requires editorial judgment. None contradicts established grammar.

### Methodological Principle (added 2026-03-29)

**When discourse-marker status (prosody) and syntactic completeness (atomic thought) conflict, syntactic completeness governs.** Prosodic breaks can override syntax only when the break is clear, significant, and does unambiguous rhetorical or structural work. This privileges the foundational test as the single governing principle, reducing the rule set from a list of exceptions to a coherent hierarchy:

1. **Syntax (atomic thought)** — always governs unless overridden
2. **Prosody (atomic breath)** — can override syntax only when break is clear and significant
3. **Editorial instinct** — flags candidates but doesn't decide
